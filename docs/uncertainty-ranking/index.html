How much of a country ranking is real?

PISA 2025. Every country table publishes a standard error beside each estimate, but rankings are read off the point estimates. This page puts confidence intervals, rank ranges and significance tests back in, and shows why inverse-variance weighting is a poor way to average countries. Choose an indicator. Several systems (Luxembourg, Macao and Malta among them) have very small standard errors on the school-level indicators, which matters a great deal for weighting.

Significance threshold

Source: OECD, PISA 2025 Results (Volume I), chapter 4; estimates and standard errors as published. Intervals are est ± 1.96 SE. Rank ranges are the 2.5th to 97.5th percentile of each system’s rank over 20,000 simulated draws from Normal(est, SE²), treating systems as independent samples. Pairwise z = (a − b) / √(se₁² + se₂²). “Bonferroni” divides 5% by the number of comparisons made against one chosen system, which is strict. Rank 1 = highest value, so for “never used AI” it is the least AI use. A published standard error is sampling error only: it says nothing about measurement or definitional differences, and near-census systems look certain because their sampling error is almost zero. Statistical significance is not practical importance. Fixed-effect inverse-variance and DerSimonian–Laird random-effects means are shown for comparison; Cochran’s I² is the share of between-system variation that is not sampling noise.
