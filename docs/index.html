Digital devices and AI in school: PISA 2025

Interactive analyses of the OECD's chapter 4 country tables. Each page is generated from the same workbook by a Python script in the repository.

  • Who uses AI and digital devices in class, and who doesn't?

    72 group gaps (gender, SES, immigrant background, education level, programme, school location and type) across nine AI and device outcomes. Select any cell for country-level gaps with confidence intervals and a written summary.

    Tables I.B1.4.26, 43, 54 to 59, 68 · script analysis/01_equity_gaps.py

  • Do richer schools really have better tech? It depends what you ask.

    Seven school-resourcing measures, split by rich/poor, city/rural and private/public schools. Raw device counts often favour poorer or rural schools, but reported shortages, poor quality and readiness to teach digitally clearly favour richer and private ones. Plain-language grid plus a per-country lookup.

    Tables I.B1.4.1, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 15 · script analysis/02_infrastructure_divides.py

  • Does school guidance keep pace with student AI use?

    Compares the share of students using AI for schoolwork with the share asked, in lessons, to assess AI output, across 85 systems. Includes a comparison with guidance on online information, a ranking, and a lookup for any system.

    Tables I.B1.4.53, 55, 67, 68 · script analysis/07_use_guidance_gap.py

  • How are schools governing devices and AI, and is it changing?

    A nine-item policy-intensity index, plus the four device-policy regimes compared 2022 vs 2025 with each system's shift tested against its own standard error and classified as a fast adopter or lagging. Includes open questions on whether phone bans track with less in-school leisure device use or less AI use.

    Tables I.B1.4.47, 49 · script analysis/08_policy_typologies.py

  • How do resources, capacity, time use and AI relate?

    Nine country-level composite indices built from resourcing, teacher capacity, time use, leisure, distraction, device rules and AI use/guidance. A correlation explorer with country-level scatterplots, plus five clustered country archetypes.

    Tables I.B1.4.1, 4, 6, 11, 14, 21, 37, 43, 47, 53, 55, 67, 68 · script analysis/09_cross_domain_panel.py + 09b + 09c
Source: OECD, PISA 2025 Results (Volume I), chapter 4 tables. Pages are rebuilt from data/digital_devices_AI_chapter4.xlsx with python build_site.py. Figures are descriptive; see each page's footnote for how estimates and significance are defined.
