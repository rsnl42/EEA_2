What do students do online for fun, and how does it change at weekends?

PISA 2025. Students reported how long they spend each day on seven digital leisure activities, on weekdays and on weekends. The OECD text only discusses the 2022 to 2025 changes; this compares activity mixes across countries, measures the weekday-to-weekend shift, and contrasts passive consumption with creative use. Only 44 of 91 systems have data in these tables: the others have none at all, on both weekdays and weekends, rather than a few suppressed cells. The 44 covered systems average 472 in science against 416 for the rest, so patterns here describe higher-scoring systems.

Source: OECD, PISA 2025 Results (Volume I), chapter 4 tables I.B1.4.37 (weekdays) and I.B1.4.40 (weekends). “Above 3 hours” sums the three top bands; whiskers are conservative 95% intervals (weekday and weekend come from the same students). Estimated hours use band midpoints (the open “more than 7 hours” band counted as 8); country rankings barely change if it is 10, but the seven activities overlap and their estimates sum to far more than a day’s device time, so read hours as a comparison, not as absolute time. “Passive” = browsing social networks + browsing the Internet for fun; “creative” = creating or editing own digital content; the other four activities are in neither group. These groupings are a judgement call.
