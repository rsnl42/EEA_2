Do students use AI to draft or to learn?

PISA 2025. Students said how often they use AI for four school purposes: summarising a text, preliminary research, drafting texts for writing assignments, and helping them learn. The OECD report gives the frequencies; this compares drafting (a rough proxy for offloading) with learning country by country, separates how many students use AI at all from how heavily those users do, and groups countries by their pattern. 85 of 91 systems are covered; no Table 4.53 values exist for Mauritius; Cambodia; Kurdistan Region (Iraq); Zambia; Dushanbe (Tajikistan); Rwanda.

Source: OECD, PISA 2025 Results (Volume I), chapter 4 table I.B1.4.53. “Weekly or more” = once or twice a week plus daily or almost daily. “Adoption” = 100 minus the % never or almost never (used at least once or twice a year). “Intensity among users” = weekly-or-more as a share of adopters; it is a ratio of two reported figures and carries no interval. Gap whiskers are conservative 95% intervals (drafting and learning come from the same students). Drafting is only a proxy for offloading: the survey records how often AI is used, not who did the thinking. Tables 4.56 to 4.59 are the never-or-almost-never share by student and school group, covered on the equity page. The three patterns differ mainly in overall level, not in the mix of purposes, and are a broad summary rather than a firm classification.
