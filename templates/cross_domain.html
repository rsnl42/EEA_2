How do digital resources, capacity, time use and AI relate?

PISA 2025, up to 91 education systems. Nine country-level composite indices, each averaging several chapter-4 indicators (see the footnote), plus mean science performance as context, not an input.

Correlation matrix

Spearman rank correlation across systems, every pair of composites plus science. Darker = stronger relationship; blue = positive, red = negative. Composites are z-scores of skewed raw indicators, so rank correlation is the safer default over Pearson. Click any cell (not on the diagonal) for that pair's country-level scatter.

Five archetypes

Ward hierarchical clustering on the 9 composites (83 systems with complete data). Silhouette scores are low at every k tested (0.14-0.18) — these are broad gradients, not tightly separated groups. See the script's docstring (analysis/09c_cross_domain_clusters.py) for how k=5 was chosen and its limits. Click a card, a dot, or use the lookup below.

Source: OECD, PISA 2025 Results (Volume I), chapter 4 tables I.B1.4.1, 4, 6, 11, 14, 21, 37, 43, 47, 53, 55, 67, 68, plus Table I.1 (science performance) for context. Each composite standardises and averages several same-direction indicators (script analysis/09_cross_domain_panel.py lists the exact indicators per composite). Country-level relationships and clusters are ecological (they describe systems, not students) and, without a GDP/wealth control in this workbook, may partly reflect national income — this applies most to anything involving resources or science. With 45 simultaneous correlation tests, some will be significant by chance; the page reports how many are, against that baseline. Systems marked * carry comparability flags in the source.
